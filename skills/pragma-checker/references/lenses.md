# Lens catalog

Five axes. Directions in the final set must come from different axes. Each lens lists the question to ask of
the text and the textual signals that count as triggers. Theory source in brackets; see sources.md.

## 1. Intent (what the text is trying to get done)
Speech acts [Austin/Searle via Eragamreddy; indirect speech in Sato's PragMega categories].
- Ask: is this an assertion on the surface but a request, refusal, warning, offer or commitment underneath?
- Signals: statements of fact addressed to someone who could act on them; questions that do not seek information; refusals phrased as reasons (Wilson & Bishop's "I'm working all day" declining a lift).
- Relevance move: the reply or statement is only relevant if it is read as a response to some nearby goal.

## 2. Face and relation (what the text does to the relationship)
Politeness [Brown & Levinson via Eragamreddy, with the Wilson & Bishop caution]. Includes polite deceit.
- Ask: given power (P), distance (D) and imposition (R), why this level of directness or softness? Who is being protected or pressured?
- Signals: hedges, softeners ("I was wondering if"), praise before a request, face-saving euphemism, unusual formality or familiarity for the pair.
- Caution: this is inference from context, not a check against etiquette rules.

## 3. Omission and epistemic stance (what is withheld, presumed or under-supported)
Gricean Quantity and Quality [Grice via Sato Appendix A].
- Ask: what would a fully informative author include here and has not? What is being presented as settled that the evidence does not settle?
- Signals: missing comparators, absent denominators, unstated agent, conspicuous silence on an obvious next point, certainty words with thin support, scalar terms ("some", "may") chosen over stronger available ones.
- State the question under discussion you assume. A stronger statement only counts as "avoided" if it would have been relevant to that question (Degen 2023).
- Scalar implicature is the near-automatic kind (generalised). Prefer particularised readings that depend on this specific context; mention generalised ones only if the context cancels or reinforces them.

## 4. Attitude and non-literal force (how the author feels or means it non-literally)
Irony, metaphor, hyperbole, emotion [Sato PragMega categories; Eragamreddy on sarcasm and affect].
- Ask: does the literal content clash with the context in a way that signals the opposite or a figurative meaning? Is emotion expressed indirectly?
- Signals: praise in a negative context, hyperbole, rhetorical question, scare quotes, capitalisation or intensity markers, a comparison whose literal reading is absurd.
- Reliability is lower on metaphor and on irony without rich context.

## 5. Sociocultural and genre frame (what conventions the text is operating under)
Context, culture, register, institutional setting [Eragamreddy; context as filter].
- Ask: what does this genre/community/period normally mean by this phrasing? What would a reader inside the frame take for granted that an outsider would miss?
- Signals: boilerplate that is code for something, jargon with an in-group sense, indirectness norms of the author's culture or institution, platform conventions, dated references.
- State the norm you assumed; a wrong cultural prior is the main failure here.

## Using the lenses

- Lenses are prompts for candidate generation, not output sections. Do not report a lens that found nothing.
- A reading can have a primary axis and a secondary one; assign the primary and keep the set orthogonal on primary axes.
- If a text is a bare list of facts with no author stance, expect the control to win.
